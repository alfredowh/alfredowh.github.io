Phone Video to a Metric Photoreal 3D Scene using Gaussian Splatting

Overview

A phone video of a real table scene (table, background, a bowl and a plate) turned into a metric, photoreal 3D model with 3D Gaussian Splatting. It started as the visual-fidelity branch of hurobo (GitHub) and is a visual result only: the splats are not used to train or evaluate a policy.

Pipeline

  1. Capture: walk around the scene with a phone. A ChArUco board shown on a tablet gives the metric frame.
  2. Structure from motion: keyframes go to pycolmap for camera poses. COLMAP's default SIFT settings registered only 39 of 120 frames of a low-texture clip, so the contrast threshold is lowered.
  3. Splat training: gsplat trains the Gaussians on the registered frames.
  4. Metric alignment: the board fixes scale and a z-up table frame with the tabletop at z = 0 (corner RMS 0.5 to 1.1 mm).
  5. Cut: crop the whole scene to a box, or cut objects out of it (bowl and plate found as blobs above the tabletop).

Result: Photo vs. Splat

Four held-out camera poses (not used in training), phone photo on the left and splat render on the right.

Held-out PSNR is 29.5 dB for one clip and 25.6 dB for the two clips combined (not a like-for-like comparison).

Scanned Objects: Bowl and Plate

The bowl and plate are cut out of the same scan. Their measured sizes against a ruler: plate about 26 cm (ruler 25.5 cm), bowl about 16 × 14 cm (ruler 13.8 cm).

The glossy white plate is textureless, so the optimiser leaves translucent patches and a warped rim, and more iterations did not help. A shape prior fills it: the measured dish (25.5 cm diameter, 2.8 cm high) is fitted to the splat, Gaussians off the surface are dropped, and flat Gaussians are added where the surface is open.

Limitations

  • One table, one board position, two clips; the table is only partly covered.
  • The tablet thickness and square size are hand measurements.
  • The cut objects carry a skirt of wood grain from the table.
  • The plate fill is opt-in, judged visually, with no quality number.

Used in hurobo, where the bowl and plate splats are shown inside the LIBERO simulator. Code: the scan pipeline lives in the hurobo repository on GitHub.
