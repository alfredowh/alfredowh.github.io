hurobo: From Phone Video of a Human Hand to a Robot Policy in Simulation

Personal project · Humanoid challenge · 8 – 16 October 2026

  • Vision-Language-Action (VLA)
  • SmolVLA

  • Real2Sim
  • Gaussian Splatting

Overview

Question: can human videos improve a small VLA on a new layout and a new object? The test case is SmolVLA (450M parameters), a bowl-onto-plate task on a Franka Panda in the LIBERO simulator, trained only on data from phone videos of a human hand. Simulation only, no physical robot.

The video is used as a motion-capture device. Markers and a hand tracker turn each clip into a hand path in the table frame. That path is retargeted to many simulated object poses, and only successful runs become training data. SmolVLA never sees a real pixel. Gaussian-splat scans of the real scene are added for visual fidelity.

Constraints

  • One 16 GB GPU: a 450M model in bf16, no 3B/7B models.
  • Phone only: about 26 tripod clips, one camera, no depth sensor, no glove.
  • No physical robot: success rate in LIBERO is the only metric.

Pipeline: Hand Video In, Robot Demos Out

  1. Record: tripod phone, ChArUco-calibrated. ArUco markers on table, bowl and plate.
  2. Extract: hand TCP (midpoint of thumb and index tip), grasp from the pinch, and object poses, all in the table frame.
  3. Twin: copy the real layout and camera into LIBERO, with a real vs. sim side-by-side replay.
  4. Multiply: split each demo into object-relative segments, retarget to many sim poses, keep only the successes.
  5. Train: fine-tune SmolVLA from smolvla_base (never saw LIBERO) on generated data only.
  6. Evaluate: lerobot-eval with success rate and 95% confidence intervals against baselines.

Who contributes what. The human video supplies approach direction, grasp timing, lift height, carry path, place offset and release timing. The simulator supplies exact object poses, geometry and appearance, with a fixed top-down gripper orientation and the yaw taken from the object.

The Human Demo

ArUco markers on the plate and the bowl pad, and a ChArUco board as the table frame. MediaPipe gives the hand skeleton; the TCP is in metres in the table frame; pinch aperture with hysteresis gives open or closed. Each clip is stored as a LeRobot episode (here: 199 samples at 20 Hz over 9.9 s).

From 26 clips to 12 usable demos

  1. Filter frames: drop frames with no hand or two hands, a marker pose that reprojects badly (over 15 px), a hand at the image border, or depth outside 0.15 to 3 m.
  2. Find the cycle: keep the first gap-free run that holds open, closed, open.
  3. Cut: trim from the start of the run to 2 s after the release.
  4. Reject: drop clips shorter than 1.5 s or missing the board, bowl or plate marker in the first second.

Generating the Robot Demos

  1. Sample layout: a random bowl and plate position in the size-matched LIBERO scene (bowl ×1.23, plate ×1.85 of LIBERO's size).
  2. Pick source: one of the 12 cleaned human demos at random per rollout.
  3. Re-anchor: the approach (last 20 cm of the hand path) relative to the bowl, the carry relative to the plate.
  4. Execute, keep wins: grasp at a fixed 4 cm height, release 4 mm over the plate, run closed loop and keep only the successes.

First run: 100 successes of 170 attempts (59 %) in about 40 to 50 minutes in one process. Failures: 37 never lifted the bowl, 33 timed out. The output is 35,498 frames at 20 Hz with agent-view and wrist cameras.

Evaluation

Pretrained SmolVLA: layout and size matter

Pretrained checkpoints across five scene variants (stock, cleared, new layout, real-size objects). A new layout alone takes the better checkpoint from 6/10 to 0/10, and real-size objects give 0 even on LIBERO's layout. 10 episodes per cell, so 0/10 means 0 to 28 % at 95 %.

  • Keep it framed as visual fidelity, not a trained result.

Future Work: Faster Demo Generation (MimicGen-style)

The generator can be sped up with a MimicGen-style expansion: pick the human source demo nearest to the sampled layout, add action noise, and screen rollouts by length. On 7 clips, the best screened run gave 100 of 125 accepted (80 %), all within the 300-step evaluation limit. The default generator accepted 38 to 47 per 100 to 120 attempts, and only 15 to 21 % of its episodes fit within 300 steps.

Pros

  • About 4× more usable episodes per attempt
  • Every episode fits the 300-step evaluation
  • Drops the 3 of 7 clips that never succeed
  • Noise and source mixing add variety

Cons

  • The gain is mostly screening, speed and the length filter
  • Control run missing
  • No policy trained on it yet
  • 7 clips, one session, one seed

This is optimization potential, not a result: it was not used for the 100-episode training set.

Limitations and Status

Limitations

  • Simulation only, no real robot
  • Hand vs. gripper, real vs. rendered pixels
  • One camera, markers required
  • Fixed top-down grasp, one task
  • 12 usable clips; marker size not measured

Done, on real clips

  • 26 clips recorded, 12 cleaned
  • 100 generated episodes (100 of 170 rollouts)
  • SmolVLA fine-tuned: 14/20 from smolvla_libero, 3/20 from smolvla_base
  • Bowl, plate and scene scanned as splats

Still open

  • N = 50 evaluation, more seeds
  • Zero-shot and open-loop replay baselines, ACT baseline
  • Scene splat and collision meshes in LIBERO
  • Training on faster MimicGen-style generation
